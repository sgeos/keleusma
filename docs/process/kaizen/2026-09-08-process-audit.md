**A software project run by one human and several concurrent language-model sessions has, since March, accumulated about two thousand commits on its version branch, has merged three hundred seventy five pull requests in the four weeks since it began using them, and has written roughly three megabytes of process prose, and it has also recorded, in its own words, more than forty distinct incidents in which its own record of itself was wrong [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]] [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]].** This article is an audit of that process. It was commissioned by the project's operator with four objectives that pull against one another, high quality engineering output, honest progress against a roadmap, as little calendar time as is realistic, and as little operator involvement as is realistic. The audit was performed by a fresh session on a dedicated branch, reading the project's three active lines through version control rather than through any of their working directories, and it treats nothing done to date, including the process documents it measures against, as presumed correct [[handoffs/kaizen.md at 7f87d03f][primary_handoff_kaizen]].

**The central finding is that the project does not have a quality problem or a speed problem in the ordinary sense.** Its continuous integration runs green about eighty seven percent of the time, its pull requests merge in a median of fifty minutes, and its lines have caught, corrected, and recorded a large number of their own errors [[CI workflow runs, sgeos/keleusma][primary_workflow_runs]] [[Merged pull requests, sgeos/keleusma][primary_pull_requests]]. What it has is a verification problem and a decision-latency problem, and the two are related. The instruments the sessions build to check their own work have failed repeatedly, and most of those failures were silent, a clean report or a still-running status where a failure should have shown. The decisions that block the largest work items wait on the operator for weeks while the process documents a resume protocol that consumes a session's first hour reading a megabyte of history. The remedies the project has recorded for both are, in the majority of cases, sentences rather than mechanisms, and the record shows the same failure class recurring after its sentence was written.

**The article proceeds from the evidence to the findings to a set of proposals, each stated with its mechanism, the evidence behind it, its expected effect on each of the four objectives, and the ruling it needs.** Nothing here is adopted. The operator rules on practice, and this document is an argument addressed to that ruling.

## What the Project Is and How It Works

**Keleusma is a total functional stream processor, a small language compiling to bytecode for a stack machine, whose selling point is a statically bounded worst case in time and memory [[CLAUDE.md at 802e72d3][primary_claude_md]].** It is developed in Rust under a no-standard-library constraint, and it is in the middle of a self-hosting effort in which a compiler written in Keleusma is checked byte for byte against the reference compiler written in Rust. That differential oracle is the project's correctness signal, and much of the process exists to protect it [[CLAUDE.md at 802e72d3][primary_claude_md]].

**The process itself is documented in about fifteen files under a process directory, and it has three moving parts.** The first is a set of communication channels between the human, called the operator or pilot, and the sessions [[COMMUNICATION.md at 802e72d3][primary_communication]]. A prompt file is written only by the human. A reverse prompt is overwritten by the session after each task. A design journal is appended to, newest first, after each increment. A task log is the shared record of sprint state. A handoff document is written before a planned context compaction and stamped with the commit it describes, so that a resuming session can validate it by ancestry before trusting it [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]] [[AUTONOMOUS_IMPLEMENTATION_LOOP.md at 802e72d3][primary_autonomous_loop]]. The second moving part is a release-branch git model. A main branch holds releases, a version branch integrates the next release, and short-lived feature branches are cut from the version branch and merged back with no-fast-forward merges after a green continuous-integration run [[GIT_STRATEGY.md at 802e72d3][primary_git_strategy]]. The third is a parallel-development protocol under which several sessions work at once, each in its own git worktree on its own branch, and communicate through per-branch markdown mailboxes read with `git show` rather than through any live channel [[PARALLEL_DEVELOPMENT.md at 802e72d3][primary_parallel_development]] [[handoffs/README.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoffs_readme]].

**At the time of the audit three lines were active and a fourth had just been opened for the audit itself.** The V0.2.X line works the version branch in the primary checkout. The V0.3.X line works a native code generation backend on a second version branch, absorbing the first line's work by numbered merges, fifty four of them so far [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]]. A proofs line produced a mathematical proof of a memory-reuse property, had it adversarially audited across three rounds by fresh contexts, and landed it on both peers [[handoffs/proofs.md at 6537a36f][primary_handoff_proofs]] [[AUDIT_2026-08-24.md at 6537a36f][primary_audit_round1]] [[AUDIT_2026-08-24_ROUND2.md at 6537a36f][primary_audit_round2]] [[AUDIT_2026-08-26_ROUND3.md at 6537a36f][primary_audit_round3]]. The lines are run by the same operator and, until the most recent sessions, by the same model family. The operator's own statement to the audit was that no assumption should be made that the work to date has been optimal or correct, nor that the piloting has been effective, and that the operator may be unaware of documented workflows for highly autonomous development.

## The Evidence Base

**Ten evidence passes were run, and their reports are preserved beside this draft.** A first pass read every process document, the continuous-integration workflow, the pre-push hook, and the script headers, and tabulated the documented pilot touchpoints, gates, contradictions, and rules that name the failure they were written after. A second pass mined the V0.2.X line's mailbox, journal, task log, reverse prompt, and handoff for its own record of failures, corrections, operator dependencies, and coordination. A third did the same for the V0.3.X line and the proofs line, including the three proof audit records and the operator-decision ledgers that line keeps. A fourth measured the git and continuous-integration record directly, every commit on four branches, all three hundred seventy five merged pull requests, and all one thousand three hundred eighty seven workflow runs. A fifth read the operator's blog repository, which runs a near-identical process for prose, as a comparison point and as the format reference for this document. A sixth surveyed the published evidence on autonomous and multi-agent software development and graded each source by what it establishes. Four further passes, run after a first draft, searched for the current state of the art matched to the findings, context and state management for long-running agents, verification of generated code and of the agents' own instruments, decision workflows and progress measurement and continuous-integration cost, and coordination substrates, and one of them re-fetched the five primary sources the first survey could not verify.

**The evidence files label claims as executed or read at the level of a pass or a section, not per table row.** A claim marked executed was produced by a command the auditor or a miner ran and whose output was read. A claim marked read is a document's own assertion, reproduced. The incident tables in the second and third evidence files carry no per-row label and are read claims throughout. The distinction matters because the project's own record shows that documents assert measurements that were never taken, and this audit is not exempt from that hazard. Where a figure came from a miner rather than from the auditor's own command, that is stated. Where a miner's figure was checked by the auditor, that is stated too. The auditor recounted the incident classes over the tabulated rows and did not re-derive the classification of any row.

**One incident during the audit belongs in the evidence.** The first attempt to run the six passes in parallel on the most capable model available was terminated after about thirty minutes by an account-level session limit, with no report surviving from any pass. The passes were rerun on a smaller model and completed. The process documents do not mention session or usage limits anywhere, and the calendar cost of that interruption, about an hour, is the kind of cost the project has no instrument for.

## Finding One, the Resume Surface Has Grown Past What a Session Can Read

**The session startup protocol in the project's root instruction file tells a new session to read the handoff, the task log, and the reverse prompt, then wait.** Measured at the audit commit, those three files on the V0.2.X line are ninety three kilobytes, four hundred thirty four kilobytes, and nineteen kilobytes. The design journal the handoff points to is one million fifty seven thousand bytes across eleven thousand lines. The V0.3.X line's handoff, which is also its mailbox, is five hundred thirty one kilobytes across eight thousand one hundred thirty six lines, and its journal is one point three megabytes [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]] [[DESIGN_JOURNAL.md on v0.3.0 at b8d78c3f][primary_design_journal_v030]]. The operator's blog repository, running the same machinery, has a task log of one point zero four megabytes and a handoff of one hundred seventy five kilobytes.

| Document, V0.2.X line | Bytes | Lines | In the startup protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HANDOFF.md` | 93,081 | 1,439 | yes, first |
| `TASKLOG.md` | 434,064 | 2,165 | yes |
| `REVERSE_PROMPT.md` | 19,096 | 307 | yes |
| `DESIGN_JOURNAL.md` | 1,056,884 | 11,096 | pointed to |
| `handoffs/v0.2.3.md` | 160,773 | 2,504 | pointed to by the handoff |

**The project has already diagnosed this class once and recorded the diagnosis.** On 2026-07-22 the reverse prompt had accreted to about three hundred sixty two kilobytes, contrary to its overwrite-each-task specification, and the journal was split off as an append-only file so the reverse prompt could stay bounded [[COMMUNICATION.md at 802e72d3][primary_communication]] [[DESIGN_JOURNAL.md at 802e72d3][primary_design_journal_v023]]. The communication document records this as process audit item five. The same accretion has since recurred in the task log, the handoff, and both mailboxes. The communication document does instruct that same-day task log entries be consolidated, a sentence rule the task log's size shows is not followed, and no other size discipline exists for these files [[COMMUNICATION.md at 802e72d3][primary_communication]] [[TASKLOG.md at 802e72d3][primary_tasklog_v023]]. The V0.2.X handoff says of itself that it is long and largely historical, that only its top banner is current, and it leaves a superseded duplicate of its own resume heading further down as an example of the drift it warns about [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]]. The V0.3.X mailbox opens with a correction to its own history and records that its banner read "after absorption 18" for three days while the body described absorption 38 [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]].

**The consequence is not only the reading cost.** A resuming session must decide how much of a megabyte to read, and the documents do not say. The one explicit reading order, in the V0.2.X handoff, lists five files and a peer mailbox to be read to the end. A session that reads all of it spends a substantial fraction of its context before doing any work. A session that reads the top banner only trusts a document the project's own record shows goes stale within days. Neither choice is documented as the intended one, and the two startup protocols the project carries, one in the root instruction file and one in the communication document, disagree on step count and content and do not reference each other [[CLAUDE.md at 802e72d3][primary_claude_md]] [[COMMUNICATION.md at 802e72d3][primary_communication]]. The volume the protocol asks a session to read before working, taking the common heuristic of about four bytes per token for English prose, is in the order of a hundred and forty thousand tokens before the journal is opened [[What are tokens and how to count them, OpenAI][ref_openai_tokens]].

$$
N_{\text{tokens}} \approx \frac{B_{\text{handoff}} + B_{\text{tasklog}} + B_{\text{reverse}}}{4} = \frac{93{,}081 + 434{,}064 + 19{,}096}{4} \approx 1.4 \times 10^{5}
$$

**The journal adds another two hundred and sixty thousand on the same heuristic, and the peer mailbox forty thousand more.** The context-degradation results in the state of the art section below find accuracy falling well before a model's stated limit, so the figure is not a question of whether the files fit but of how much of what was read the session can then use.

## Finding Two, Stale Figures and Fabricating Instruments Are the Largest Failure Classes

**Across the two engineering lines' own records the miners tabulated forty three distinct incidents in two tables, twenty two on the V0.2.X line and twenty one on the V0.3.X and proofs side, each with a root-cause class.** The auditor recounted the classes over those rows, counting a row with two classes under the first named. The miners also ran keyword sweeps that returned several hundred hits not each triaged and reported approximate tallies from them. Those tallies are larger, are marked approximate in the evidence files, and are not combined with the row counts here. The totals over the tabulated rows are in the table.

| Root cause class | V0.2.X | V0.3.X and proofs | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| tooling fabrication, an instrument reporting what it did not measure | 5 | 4 | 9 |
| stale count or stale document | 3 | 5 | 8 |
| checker reach, a clean check that did not cover the case | 4 | 2 | 6 |
| gate or continuous-integration blind spot | 5 | 1 | 6 |
| model overconfidence | 4 | 2 | 6 |
| relayed ruling or provenance | 1 | 4 | 5 |
| specification misread | 0 | 3 | 3 | Each class's share of the tabulated population is its row count over the total.

$$
s_{c} = \frac{n_{c}}{N} \qquad s_{\text{tooling}} = \frac{9}{43} = 0.209 \qquad s_{\text{stale}} = \frac{8}{43} = 0.186 \qquad s_{\text{tooling}} + s_{\text{stale}} = \frac{17}{43} = 0.395
$$

**Two classes account for two fifths of the tabulated incidents, and the four classes that concern the project's own instruments and figures, tooling, stale figures, checker reach, and gate blind spots, account for twenty nine of forty three, about two thirds.** The ranking between the first two is within the miners' stated imprecision.

**The stale-figure and instrument classes are the largest by either count, the ranking between them is within the miners' stated imprecision, and the project knows both.** The V0.2.X handoff says the line has been bitten seven times by carrying a figure forward instead of re-deriving it, most recently a source comment citing offsets an order of magnitude wrong [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]]. The construct-support boundary count, the line's headline progress metric, was wrong three times running because each correction adjusted the previous figure rather than re-summing per group, and re-summing exposed a missing group. The V0.3.X line found that a site count of two hundred thirty nine had no live producer and was really two hundred fifty six, that a corpus population of ninety one was really sixty seven because three test files both listed a directory and recursed into it, and that three module counts in three places were all correct and none labelled with what it counted [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]] [[STALE_COUNT_TRIAGE.md at b8d78c3f][primary_stale_count_triage]]. The root instruction file carried a wrong claim about a cargo feature set until 2026-08-16, and its contributor guide still says the project follows trunk-based development, a model the git strategy document explicitly superseded [[CONTRIBUTING.md at 802e72d3][primary_contributing]] [[GIT_STRATEGY.md at 802e72d3][primary_git_strategy]].

**The project's own remedy for this class has the correct shape and is not applied to its own documents.** The V0.2.X line wrote a test that fails when the test counts stated in the root instruction file disagree with the measured counts, after finding that file's counts wrong by a factor of three [[tests/claimed_counts.rs at 802e72d3][primary_claimed_counts]]. It wrote another that forces the boundary figure to appear twice in the handoff so a stale copy turns the suite red. That control was itself defeated once, on 2026-09-03, when a handoff refresh deleted one of the two occurrences, and the deletion was invisible because the pre-push hook's routine tier excludes the test binaries that would have caught it [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]] [[.cargo-husky/hooks/pre-push at 802e72d3][primary_pre_push]]. The pattern is that a figure written into prose by a session is a liability from the moment it is written, that the project has learned this, and that its handoffs, mailboxes, and instruction files continue to be written by hand with figures in them.

## Finding Three, the Instruments Fail, and Mostly Fail Silent

**Nine tabulated incidents are of an instrument reporting a result it did not measure, and most of them were silent.** The V0.2.X handoff records two distinct sets from two sessions [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]]. One is a set of silent failures the handoff counts as seven, a timeout command absent on the operating system that made a gate a no-op, an unconditional echo of a passing lint, a pipe through tail that masked an exit status of one hundred one, and status waiters that misread a still-running job. The handoff's summary of that set is that none erred toward a false alarm. The other is a table of five constructed statuses from a different session, and two of those did err toward alarm, a monitor and a shell each reporting jobs failed that were in fact still running. On 2026-09-05 three of a session's own instruments reported confident verdicts about things they never touched, a sweep column listing a warning under passing, a mutation test aimed at the wrong call site, and a probe that mutated an opcode absent from its own program and reported it admitted. The record says none was caught by review [[REVERSE_PROMPT.md at 802e72d3][primary_reverse_prompt_v023]].

**The V0.3.X line's mutation census is the clearest single case.** The census document said no hole was open. The harness classified a mutation as detected whenever the test process exited non-zero, so after a test change on 2026-08-21 it would have scored every module as detecting every mutation regardless of truth. A sweep under that harness ran for twelve hours fifty one minutes, completed five of twenty five mutations before being killed, and projected about sixty hours for a full run. When the defect was found and the harness repaired, the same sweep completed in forty two minutes and showed that nine mutations covering fifty two sites had silently stopped placing after an emitter signature change, which under the old harness would have been misreported as a real hole, a false alarm of the other kind. The line's own conclusion was that the instrument was fabricating coverage and was four hundred times too slow, and the repair separated population guards from consistency guards and proved the fix in both directions [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]]. The sixty-hour projection the line made is the partial run scaled by the fraction of mutations it had covered, and the four-hundred-fold figure follows from the control measurement.

$$
T_{\text{full}} = T_{\text{partial}} \, \frac{M}{m} = 12.85\,\text{h} \times \frac{25}{5} \approx 64\,\text{h} \qquad f_{\text{detecting}} = \frac{T_{\text{real}} - T_{\text{null}}}{T_{\text{real}}} = \frac{385 - 387}{385} \approx 0
$$

**A module took 385 seconds to sweep and a module that did not exist took 387, so the fraction of the sweep's cost that could have detected anything is indistinguishable from zero.** The repaired sweep ran in forty two minutes, which against the projected sixty four hours is a ratio of about ninety on wall clock, and the line's own four-hundred-fold figure counts per-mutation cost rather than the whole run.

**The pattern generalizes beyond this project, and the project's own aphorism for it is the right one.** A clean report is evidence about the checker's reach before it is evidence about the tree. The blog repository's verification-traps document records the same lesson from the prose side, a reported zero errors measured against a directory that no longer existed, and a checker rewritten three times because version two masked the first error [[VERIFICATION_TRAPS.md, sgeos.github.io at 68182f3][primary_blog_verification_traps]]. The same process-lookup deadlock, a wait loop matching its own command line, appears independently in both repositories. Each repository learned it separately and wrote it down separately.

## Finding Four, Lessons Are Recorded as Sentences and as Mechanisms, and Both Recur

**The first evidence pass tabulated thirteen rules in the process documents that name the specific failure they were written after.** Of those, nine shipped with a mechanism and four are sentences, on the auditor's classification, which counts a policy that changes what gates a merge as a sentence. The journal split, the performance canary, the root ignore file, the gate status script reading log times, the documentation build in the gate and hook and workflow, the self-host job, the gate summary script, the orphan reaper, and the concurrency group are mechanisms. The rule pinning mailboxes to version branches, the superset rule for continuous integration, the policy making continuous integration authoritative for feature merges, and the release doctrine against self-certification are sentences [[PARALLEL_DEVELOPMENT.md at 802e72d3][primary_parallel_development]] [[PROCESS_STRATEGY.md at 802e72d3][primary_process_strategy]] [[GIT_STRATEGY.md at 802e72d3][primary_git_strategy]] [[RELEASE_PROCESS.md at 802e72d3][primary_release_process]]. Several of the rules the lines rely on most, derive figures rather than copy them, never record a relayed ruling as settled, and prove a checker's reach before trusting its report, live in handoffs and mailboxes rather than in the process documents, and are sentences there. The mechanism share is the count over the thirteen.

$$
s_{\text{mechanism}} = \frac{9}{13} \approx 0.69 \qquad s_{\text{sentence}} = \frac{4}{13} \approx 0.31
$$

**Seven of every ten reactive rules in the process documents shipped with something that runs, and the rules the lines rely on most day to day live outside those documents and did not.**

**Both forms recur, and the difference the record shows is in how the recurrence was found.** The frozen-measurement discipline on the V0.3.X line was violated four times across three sessions, and the mailbox states that the last two violations came after the rule was recorded, which is why that line replaced the rule with a script [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]]. Stale figures recurred seven times on one line by that line's own count. The relayed-ruling class produced ten incidents across the lines, but the proofs line's rule against it was written on 2026-08-27 and only the incident of 2026-09-02 follows it, so the record shows one recurrence after that rule rather than many. Mechanisms failed too. The test forcing a figure to appear twice in the handoff was defeated on 2026-09-03 by a refresh that deleted one occurrence, and a performance canary asserted its ceiling only after the timed call returned, so it could not fail, and spun for fifty seven minutes instead [[REVERSE_PROMPT.md at 802e72d3][primary_reverse_prompt_v023]]. The distinction the audit can support is narrower than mechanism against sentence. The mechanism failures were found by a test going red or a figure failing to reconcile, and the sentence failures were found by a session reading its own record. The V0.3.X line's own conclusion from the mutation repair, that the fault was repaired structurally and not by resolving to be careful, is the project's inference, and the audit adopts it as a hypothesis rather than a result.

**The blog repository holds a more consolidated artifact for this.** Its verification-traps document has twenty one entries, and each entry carries the cause, the check that would have caught it, and the habit. The engineering repository has no single equivalent. The proofs handoff keeps a section of retractions kept because their causes generalize, and the V0.3.X line keeps decision documents on scope deletion and stale-count triage, but the lessons are distributed across the journal, the handoffs, the strategy document, the decisions directory, and the root instruction file, in prose, and a fresh session encounters them only if it reads the megabyte [[SCOPE_DELETION.md at b8d78c3f][primary_scope_deletion]] [[STALE_COUNT_TRIAGE.md at b8d78c3f][primary_stale_count_triage]] [[handoffs/proofs.md at 6537a36f][primary_handoff_proofs]].

## Finding Five, the Operator Is the Long Pole on the Largest Items, and Rulings Arrive by Relay

**The documents define twenty points at which the operator is required, and the pattern among them is clean.** Defaults exist for ordering and pacing. The autonomous implementation loop says proceeding on an obvious increment is the default, that silence means continue, and it enumerates and rejects the rationalizations a session might use to stop early [[AUTONOMOUS_IMPLEMENTATION_LOOP.md at 802e72d3][primary_autonomous_loop]]. No default exists for anything irreversible, for semantics, or for self-certification, and that is deliberate and, in the audit's assessment, correct. A publish, a version bump of the bytecode format, a new opcode, or a breaking public interface change waits for a word [[RELEASE_PROCESS.md at 802e72d3][primary_release_process]] [[CLAUDE.md at 802e72d3][primary_claude_md]].

**The items that wait are the ones that block the most work.** The V0.2.X reverse prompt names two questions as the ones that block everything large, how a bounded text value is constructed, and whether an address-width change justifies breaking thirty three signatures on a published crate. Both were open at the close of session sixty three [[REVERSE_PROMPT.md at 802e72d3][primary_reverse_prompt_v023]] [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]]. A ten-line verifier repair for a real soundness hole has been pinned by a test and deferred since 2026-09-04. A proof result that would lower a published worst-case memory figure is unruled in either direction since 2026-08-24, and the handoff is careful to say that unruled must not be read as declined [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]] [[COMPOSITE_REGION_REUSE_PROOF.md at 6537a36f][primary_proof]]. Publication has been held throughout the period with no start date and no lifting criterion recorded anywhere. Four items relayed on 2026-08-08 were confirmed in session on 2026-08-31, twenty three days later.

**Rulings reach the lines through the wrong channel.** A ruling is given in one session, in prose, to one line. That line writes it into its mailbox. A peer line reads the mailbox and acts, or relays it further. The proofs line recorded a relayed rebase ruling as settled in its mailbox and in the proof's appendix, and its own operator's word had been merge. The proofs handoff records that three operator sessions gave three words for the same question, merge, rebase, and sync [[handoffs/proofs.md at 6537a36f][primary_handoff_proofs]]. That is not only a relay failure. The same operator, asked in three sessions, answered three ways, and no artifact existed in which the first answer could be read before the second was given. The V0.3.X line told the operator a source file had no owner after reading a peer's handoff backwards on the word "they", and the V0.2.X line relayed the same misreading to its own operator session without checking both texts. On the V0.3.X tip two contradictory records of one string-interface ruling coexisted, one dated 2026-08-20 and one dated 2026-08-29, and the line correctly flagged rather than reconciled them because reconciling another line's record was not its call [[DESIGN_JOURNAL.md on v0.3.0 at b8d78c3f][primary_design_journal_v030]] [[ABI_RULINGS.md at b8d78c3f][primary_abi_rulings]]. The V0.3.X line has since built the right artifact, a rulings ledger under its decisions directory with verbatim quotes, dates, the line's own reading marked as its own, and a separate open-decisions page with option tables [[ABI_RULINGS.md at b8d78c3f][primary_abi_rulings]] [[OPERATOR_DECISIONS_OPEN.md at b8d78c3f][primary_operator_decisions]]. It exists on one branch only.

**A batched packet exists in the record, and it shows both the benefit and the hazard.** On 2026-08-24 the V0.2.X line put eight numbered questions to the operator in one session and received seven rulings, recorded as a numbered table [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]]. Its third item is unruled fifteen days later, and its eighth item is the rebase ruling that was then relayed to both lines and turned out to disagree with what the same operator told the proofs line. The packet cost the operator one session for seven decisions, which is the efficient shape, and one of the seven was wrong on arrival because it was written down by the receiving line rather than by the operator. The rest of the record is single questions, each costing a session boundary, and the line's own lesson from 2026-08-19 is that two of its questions that session were already stale because it had not read the tree first, and that a wrong question costs a ruling [[TASKLOG.md at 802e72d3][primary_tasklog_v023]]. The operator's cost per decision under a packet is the cost of a session over the number of decisions it carries, and the record has the two extremes.

$$
c_{d} = \frac{C_{\text{session}}}{n_{d}} \qquad c_{d}^{\text{packet}} = \frac{C_{\text{session}}}{7} \qquad c_{d}^{\text{single}} = \frac{C_{\text{session}}}{1}
$$

**The packet form divides the operator's cost per decision by seven, and the relay error inside that packet shows the price of letting the receiving line, rather than the operator, write the answer down.** The twenty three day wait on the four relayed items is the other bound, the cost of a decision that is not packeted and not written where it can be read.

## Finding Six, Calendar Time Is Bound by the Test Job and by Documentation-Only Runs

**The measured figures from the git and workflow record are in the table, and all are executed by the metrics miner and reproducible from the raw data beside this draft [[Merged pull requests, sgeos/keleusma][primary_pull_requests]] [[CI workflow runs, sgeos/keleusma][primary_workflow_runs]].**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| pull request window | 2026-08-11 to 2026-09-08, twenty eight days |

**The median pull request cycle time equals the median workflow run time.** Merges are gated on continuous integration, and the audit infers from that cycle time, and from a standing authorization recorded in the auditor's own session memory on 2026-08-20 that routine feature merges proceed without asking, that no human review sits between a green run and a merge. No review data was pulled, and that is an inference rather than a measurement. So the calendar cost of an increment is the run time of the slowest job plus the time to open the next one, and the slowest job is the default-features test job, which has grown from twenty four minutes to sixty over the sampled period as the suite grew. The workflow sets no job timeout, so four runs in August ran into the platform's six-hour default and were cancelled, having produced nothing [[.github/workflows/ci.yml at 802e72d3][primary_ci_yml]] [[Workflow syntax, timeout-minutes, GitHub Docs][ref_github_timeout]]. An increment's calendar cost is the run time of the slowest job plus the run's fixed overhead plus the time to open the next pull request, and the measured figures put numbers on the first two terms.

$$
T_{\text{run}} = \max_{j} T_{j} + T_{\text{overhead}} \qquad T_{\text{overhead}} \approx 47 - 39 = 8\,\text{min}
$$

**The slowest job sets the run, and it has been growing.** On the five-run sample the default-features test job went from twenty four minutes on 2026-07-24 to sixty on 2026-09-08, which over forty six days is a rate that, extrapolated linearly and on five points only, reaches ninety minutes in about five more weeks.

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}T_{\text{test}}}{\mathrm{d}t} \approx \frac{60 - 24}{46} \approx 0.78\,\text{min per day} \qquad t_{90} \approx \frac{90 - 60}{0.78} \approx 38\,\text{days}
$$

**Sharding the job across runners divides its wall clock by the shard count plus a per-shard setup cost, so four shards would bring a sixty minute job to about twenty.**

$$
T_{\text{sharded}} \approx \frac{T_{\text{job}}}{k} + T_{\text{setup}} \approx \frac{60}{4} + 5 = 20\,\text{min}
$$

**That is the one lever that stops merge latency tracking suite growth, and the suite will keep growing, since the project's tests are where its evidence lives.**

**Every pull request titled as documentation pays the full run.** At least eighty five pull requests were titled as documentation changes, and each occupied a forty seven minute run of a twenty two job matrix to verify prose that most of the matrix does not read, apart from the two link-checking jobs, the documentation build, and the few test binaries that read documents. Whether each touched only documentation was not checked by file list, and the record contains one documentation commit that swept in a test file [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]]. The process itself generates these. Every handoff refresh, every mailbox update on a feature branch, and every session-close note is a pull request. Commits mentioning the handoff or mailbox are six point four percent of each trunk, and commits prefixed as process documentation are one in seven. The runner time those runs consumed, and the time the four stuck runs consumed before the platform cancelled them, are both simple products.

$$
R_{\text{docs}} = N_{\text{docs}} \, T_{\text{run}} = 85 \times 47\,\text{min} \approx 67\,\text{h} \qquad R_{\text{stuck}} = 4 \times 360\,\text{min} = 24\,\text{h}
$$

**Sixty seven runner hours went to verifying prose over four weeks, and twenty four more went to four runs that had already hung.** Neither figure is wall clock, since runs proceed in parallel, but each documentation pull request still waited its forty seven minutes, and a one-line job timeout would have returned the twenty four hours.

**Process prose and source were written at the same order of magnitude over the last sixty days, and which is larger depends on the measure.** Gross additions under the process directory were 33,643 lines against 29,407 under the source directory, of which 8,508 are Keleusma stage sources rather than Rust. Net of deletions the process directory grew by 18,137 lines, the source directory by 25,105, and the detached compiler subproject by a further 16,964. Nearly half of the gross process additions are churn on two files that are overwritten by design, and the relocation of 362 kilobytes from the reverse prompt into the journal falls inside the window. The supportable statement is that the coordination protocol writes prose at a rate comparable to the rate at which the project writes code. That is not itself a defect. Much of the prose is the record this audit was able to mine, and a project that did not write it would have nothing to learn from. It is, however, the honest measure of what the protocol costs. The parallel-development document argues from a queueing identity that a better channel buys little while the gate dominates, and it was right about the gate when the local gate took two and a half hours [[PARALLEL_DEVELOPMENT.md at 802e72d3][primary_parallel_development]] [[PROCESS_STRATEGY.md at 802e72d3][primary_process_strategy]]. The gate is now forty seven minutes and one commit in seven is process documentation. The two ratios are the gross and the net.

$$
\frac{\Delta_{\text{process}}^{\text{gross}}}{\Delta_{\text{source}}^{\text{gross}}} = \frac{33{,}643}{29{,}407} = 1.14 \qquad \frac{\Delta_{\text{process}}^{\text{net}}}{\Delta_{\text{source}}^{\text{net}}} = \frac{18{,}137}{25{,}105} = 0.72
$$

**Gross, the process directory grew faster than the source directory, and net of the churn on two overwritten files, the source directory grew faster.** The parallel-development document argues from a queueing identity that a better channel buys little while the gate dominates, and the identity is worth writing down, because the numbers in it have moved [[PARALLEL_DEVELOPMENT.md at 802e72d3][primary_parallel_development]] [[Amdahl's law][ref_amdahl]] [[Validity of the single processor approach to achieving large scale computing capabilities][research_amdahl_1967]].

$$
S_{\max} = \frac{1}{1 - f_{c}} \qquad f_{c} \approx 0.14 \;\Rightarrow\; S_{\max} \approx 1.16
$$

**Taking the process-documentation share of commits as a proxy for the coordination fraction, the identity says a perfect channel could speed the line by at most sixteen percent, which is the document's point, and it was made when the gate took two and a half hours.** The gate is now forty seven minutes, and the earlier session that ran roughly twenty full gates where four would have sufficed spent the difference [[PROCESS_STRATEGY.md at 802e72d3][primary_process_strategy]].

$$
W_{\text{gate}} = (20 - 4) \times 2.5\,\text{h} = 40\,\text{h}
$$

**Forty hours of exclusive machine time in one session went to gates that answered a question already answered, and that is the kind of figure the process now prevents by construction, since continuous integration runs off the machine.**

## Finding Seven, Progress Is Measured by Activity Except Where It Is Measured Well

**Session closes announce merge counts and test counts.** Ten merges this session [[TASKLOG.md at 802e72d3][primary_tasklog_v023]]. Routine tier green at two thousand two hundred twelve tests. These are activity measures. They rise when work is done and they also rise when work is redone, and the record contains a residency projection refuted by a units error, published and retracted, two commits withdrawn by a third, that contributed three commits to the record [[TASKLOG.md at 802e72d3][primary_tasklog_v023]].

**Two outcome measures exist and both are good.** The construct-support boundary table on the V0.2.X line counts, per construct, whether the self-hosted compiler agrees with the reference, refuses, diverges, or is rejected by the reference, and it has moved from eighty three to ninety six agreeing constructs of one hundred one over the line's history. It is pinned by a test [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]]. The V0.3.X line states a predicted test-count and conflict-count movement before every absorption and measures the actual movement after, and records partial holds explicitly, such as a prediction of one hundred sixteen binaries against an actual one hundred eighteen attributed to a concurrent absorption [[handoffs/v0.3.0.md at b8d78c3f][primary_mailbox_v030]]. Those are the two places in the record where a reader can tell from the document alone whether the project is where it says it is. The boundary table's headline is a fraction, and so are the two continuous-integration figures a reader might take as outcome measures.

$$
p_{\text{agree}} = \frac{96}{101} = 0.950 \qquad p_{\text{green}} = \frac{1{,}209}{1{,}387} = 0.872 \qquad p_{\text{recover}} = \frac{54}{110} = 0.491
$$

**Ninety five percent of constructs agree, eighty seven percent of runs are green, and half of the failures were followed by a green run on the same branch within a day.** Only the first is an outcome measure of the roadmap. The other two measure the gate.

**The roadmap itself has no such measure.** The V0.2.X handoff's macro position states that none of the five success criteria for the release line hold yet [[HANDOFF.md at 802e72d3][primary_handoff_v023]]. There is no document that lists the criteria as checkable items with their current status, and the release has been held indefinitely with no criterion for lifting the hold. The blog repository shows the same shape at a larger scale, fifty six drafted articles in a series of seventy two, none published, under a standing directive quoted verbatim on every review prompt that says do not yet publish [[HANDOFF.md, sgeos.github.io at 68182f3][primary_blog_handoff]] [[TASKLOG.md, sgeos.github.io at 68182f3][primary_blog_tasklog]]. In both repositories the outward-facing gate is where things stop, and stopping there is a deliberate posture the operator owns. The parallel is one of shape rather than of cause, since publishing prose and publishing a crate carry different risks. What the process lacks is a written statement of what would open the gate, so that the sessions could work toward it rather than around it.

## Finding Eight, Verification Has No Second Context Except for the Proof

**The proof was audited by fresh contexts across three rounds, and the audit found nine converging findings in round one, a defective repair in round two, and two soundness holes in round three [[AUDIT_2026-08-24.md at 6537a36f][primary_audit_round1]] [[AUDIT_2026-08-24_ROUND2.md at 6537a36f][primary_audit_round2]] [[AUDIT_2026-08-26_ROUND3.md at 6537a36f][primary_audit_round3]].** That is the strongest verification result in the record, and it was applied to a mathematical document. Code increments are verified by the test suite and by the session that wrote them. The pull request cycle time of fifty minutes leaves no room for a review, and the record of merges shows none. The self-hosted compiler's byte-identity oracle is a second context of a kind, and it is why the project can say that on 2026-08-31 the reference compiler, not the self-hosted one, was the divergent side on string literals with characters outside the American Standard Code for Information Interchange range. But it covers only the compiler, and only on inputs the corpus contains, which is why that defect survived until a probe census was written [[DESIGN_JOURNAL.md at 802e72d3][primary_design_journal_v023]].

**The external evidence on this point is thin but consistent.** No study measures whether adversarial review by a fresh model context catches defects at a rate comparable to human review. The one documented case of orchestrated fresh contexts outperforming a single context is Anthropic's research system, at fifteen times the token cost. The project has produced its own evidence, on the proof, that three rounds of fresh-context audit found real defects a single author did not. It has not measured a catch rate on code.

## The Contemporary Literature

**The literature was surveyed in two passes, a first for evidence about outcomes and a second for techniques matched to the findings, and every source either pass read is discussed in this section.** Each source carries a grade. Grade A is a controlled experiment or a peer-reviewed measurement. Grade B is a documented practice with reported outcomes or telemetry. Grade C is a documented practice without outcome data. Grade D is opinion. The grades are the survey agents' and the auditor has not regraded any source. Sources the argument above uses appear here again in their setting, and sources it does not use appear here only, so that a reader has the whole field and not the part of it that happened to fit.

### Controlled Experiments

**Four controlled or quasi-controlled studies bear on the calendar-time objective, and they disagree.** The Microsoft trial of 2023 randomized seventy programmers on a narrow single-file task and found the treatment group fifty six percent faster [[The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity, Evidence from GitHub Copilot][research_peng_copilot]]. The three-firm field experiment by Cui and colleagues randomized access for 4,867 developers at Microsoft, Accenture, and a Fortune 100 manufacturer and found completed tasks up twenty six percent, with larger effects for less experienced developers, though the paper was reached in this survey only through summaries of its abstract [[The Effects of Generative AI on High Skilled Work, Evidence from Three Field Experiments with Software Developers][research_cui_field]]. The Model Evaluation and Threat Research trial of 2025, METR, randomized sixteen experienced maintainers across 246 real issues in large repositories and found them nineteen percent slower with tool access while they believed themselves twenty percent faster [[Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity, METR][ref_metr_study]] [[Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity][research_metr_arxiv]]. The Carnegie Mellon difference-in-differences study over about eight hundred repositories that adopted Cursor found lines added up 281 percent in the first month and no significant velocity gain after the second, with static analysis warnings up thirty percent and complexity up forty one, both persistent [[Speed at the Cost of Quality, How Cursor AI Increases Short-Term Velocity and Long-Term Complexity in Open-Source Projects][research_he_cursor]]. All four are grade A. The disagreement is not a contradiction, since the tasks differ in size and the developers in experience, and the pattern across them is that gains are largest on small tasks for less experienced people and can reverse on large tasks for experts.

### Telemetry and Surveys

**The large observational sources agree on a single shape, individual throughput up and delivery quality flat or down.** The DevOps Research and Assessment program, DORA, reported in 2024 that over a third of respondents saw moderate to extreme productivity gains and that three quarters used the tools daily [[Accelerate State of DevOps 2024, DORA][ref_dora_2024]], and in 2025, over nearly five thousand professionals, that epics completed per developer rose sixty six percent while adoption correlated with higher delivery instability, framing the tools as a mirror and a multiplier of existing practice [[State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025, DORA][ref_dora_2025]]. Its companion capabilities model names seven organizational capabilities that predict whether adoption helps, small batch size among them, though the list reached this survey through secondary summaries [[AI Capabilities Model, DORA][ref_dora_capabilities]]. GitClear's analysis of 211 million changed lines from 2020 to 2024 found copy-pasted code rising from eight to twelve percent of changed lines and refactoring falling from a quarter to under a tenth [[AI Assistant Code Quality Research, GitClear][ref_gitclear_2025]], and its 2026 edition over 623 million changes found block duplication up eighty one percent, cross-file reuse down thirty five, refactoring commits down from twenty one percent in 2022 to under four, and maintenance of code older than a year down seventy four percent [[The Maintainability Gap, GitClear][ref_gitclear_2026]]. Uplevel's telemetry over about eight hundred developers found no cycle-time gain and forty one percent more bugs [[AI for Developer Productivity, Uplevel][ref_uplevel]]. Faros AI's 2025 report over ten thousand developers found pull request review time up ninety one percent and pull request size up 154 percent with no correlation to company-level delivery [[The AI Productivity Paradox, Faros AI][ref_faros_2025]], and its 2026 report over twenty two thousand found median review time up fivefold, bugs per pull request up twenty eight percent, incidents per pull request tripled, and churn up tenfold [[The Acceleration Whiplash, Faros AI][ref_faros_2026]]. All of these are grade B, since none isolates authorship by a model from the other changes of the period, and GitClear in particular infers attribution rather than demonstrating it.

**Three further sources characterize how the tools are used rather than what they yield.** Anthropic's analysis of four hundred thousand interactive Claude Code sessions found users making about seventy percent of planning decisions and twenty percent of execution decisions, with the explicit caveat that non-interactive and autonomous usage is substantial and unmeasured [[Agentic coding and persistent returns to expertise, Anthropic][ref_anthropic_expertise]]. A survey of eighty six practitioners running production agents across twenty six domains found sixty eight percent of agents taking ten or fewer steps before human intervention and a strong preference for static workflows over open-ended autonomy [[Measuring Agents in Production][research_agents_in_production]]. Both are grade B. An industry study reported at one remove found pull requests per engineer up ninety eight percent and, once pull request size was controlled for, no independent effect of model authorship on defect rate, which if it holds locates the risk in batch size rather than in authorship [[AI Generates Larger Pull Requests, Larger Pull Requests Bring More Bugs, ShiftMag][ref_shiftmag_pr_size]]. It is grade C and unverified at the primary. A single vendor's single customer reported thirty-day revert rates of 0.2 percent for model-assisted code against fifteen percent for human-only code, a figure the vendor itself cautions against generalizing and which this audit takes as a metric category rather than a number [[AI Code Quality, 0.2 percent vs. 15.16 percent Revert Rates, Larridin][ref_larridin_revert]]. A security report from Veracode was cited by a secondary source as finding a known flaw in forty five percent of generated samples across more than 150 models, and since the primary was not fetched it is recorded here as unverified and not cited. Sonar's synthesis of verification debt cites the Carnegie Mellon study for a velocity spike that reverses within months as issues rise, and its framing, that verification capacity has not scaled with generation capacity, is the one the audit adopts for finding eight [[AI Code Verification Debt, Sonar][ref_sonar_verification_debt]].

### Multi-Agent Frameworks and Benchmarks

**The framework papers establish capability under favorable conditions and say little about workflow reliability.** MetaGPT structures agents as product manager, architect, engineer, and quality assurance roles communicating through documents and reports full completion on its own benchmark [[MetaGPT, Meta Programming for a Multi-Agent Collaborative Framework][research_metagpt]], and ChatDev reports outperforming it on a quality metric through richer natural-language coordination [[ChatDev, Communicative Agents for Software Development][research_chatdev]]. Both are grade B on self-selected, self-scored benchmarks. SWE-agent and OpenHands are the open platforms on which most agent benchmarking now runs [[SWE-agent, Agent-Computer Interfaces Enable Automated Software Engineering][research_swe_agent]] [[OpenHands, An Open Platform for AI Software Developers as Generalist Agents][research_openhands]], and the most important benchmark result for this audit is the collapse from sixty five to seventy three percent on single-file SWE-bench Verified tasks to twenty one to twenty five percent on SWE-EVO's long-horizon tasks, which average twenty one modified files and 874 validating tests each [[SWE-EVO, Benchmarking Coding Agents in Long-Horizon Software Evolution Scenarios][research_swe_evo]]. The AI Scientist system produced three workshop submissions end to end, one of which scored above the acceptance threshold [[The AI Scientist-v2, Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search][research_ai_scientist_v2]], and Sakana's own account establishes that all were withdrawn by a protocol agreed with the conference in advance [[The AI Scientist Generates its First Peer-Reviewed Scientific Publication, Sakana AI][ref_sakana_first_publication]].

**Four 2026 studies measure coordination among coding agents directly, and they are the strongest evidence the survey found on the project's own architecture.** A Carnegie Mellon study of asynchronous engineering agents finds that a central manager building a dependency graph and delegating to agents in isolated git worktrees with test-gated merges beats single agents and looser multi-agent arrangements by 25.6 points on one benchmark and 14.7 on another [[Effective Strategies for Asynchronous Software Engineering Agents][research_caid]]. A study of 1,902 runs modelled as temporal networks finds direct messaging growing near-quadratically with team size before plateauing into broadcast, shared files cutting output tokens by about forty two percent at eight agents, no reliable improvement from designating a coordinator, and agents attempting to reach hidden grading material in about four fifths of runs [[When Agents Coordinate, Measuring Coordination in Multi-Agent AI Coding][research_when_agents_coordinate]]. A mining study of a git-native, signed, append-only coordination log finds advisory leases raising goodput from 2.33 to 3.84 distinct tasks per round and shared task state driving duplicate work from seventy eight percent to zero and goodput to 8.00, with lock starvation and a race to close the same task as the named failure modes [[Before the Pull Request, Mining Multi-Agent Coordination][research_before_the_pr]]. A dataset of 142,000 agent-authored pull requests from 59,000 repositories, 107,000 of them replayed by merge simulation, finds a 27.67 percent merge-conflict rate varying by agent [[AgenticFlict, A Large-Scale Dataset of Merge Conflicts in Agentic Pull Requests][research_agenticflict]]. The first is grade A and the other three grade B, with the caveats that peer-review status is unconfirmed for all three preprints and that the coordination-log study does not say whether its agents were live sessions. Two blackboard-architecture papers describe a central shared state to which agents post and from which they read, with the decision of which agent acts pushed to the agents themselves [[LLM-based Multi-Agent Blackboard System for Information Discovery in Data Science][research_blackboard_ds]] [[Exploring Advanced LLM Multi-Agent Systems Based on Blackboard Architecture][research_blackboard_arch]]. They are grade C, read at the abstract, and they describe the project's mailboxes in their primitive form, a polled append-only board without structured state or leases. Two further papers, one on a conflict-free replicated workspace for concurrent agents and one on cohesion-aware task partitioning, were located and not read.

### Practitioner Accounts

**The practitioner literature is grade C and D, and it is where the patterns closest to the project's own live.** Cognition argued in 2025 that parallel subagents without shared context make incompatible decisions, illustrated by two agents producing mismatched game assets, and recommended a single thread with context compression [[Don't Build Multi-Agents, Cognition][ref_cognition_multi_agents]]. Its 2026 reversal toward a coordinator managing isolated instances reached the survey only through aggregators and is recorded as unverified. Simon Willison's account of running up to four agents in separate checkouts finds human review speed the binding constraint and reports exhaustion by late morning [[Embracing the parallel coding agent lifestyle, Simon Willison][ref_willison_parallel]]. Harper Reed's brainstorm, specification, and stepwise plan before any code is the ancestor of the spec-driven tools [[My LLM codegen workflow atm, Harper Reed][ref_reed_workflow]]. Geoffrey Huntley's loop re-invokes one prompt file against a fresh context each iteration with progress in a file and in git [[Ralph Wiggum as a software engineer, Geoffrey Huntley][ref_ralph]]. The Manus team treats the file system as memory and documents recitation, a small state file rewritten in place and re-read each turn, and keeps failed actions visible so the agent does not repeat them [[Context Engineering for AI Agents, Manus][ref_manus_context]]. Steve Yegge's Beads is a git-backed issue tracker giving agents persistent dependency-aware task memory [[Introducing Beads, Steve Yegge][ref_yegge_beads]], and his Gas Town orchestrates twenty to thirty agents over it with named roles and merge-queue management, self-reported and by the author's own framing three weeks old at release [[Welcome to Gas Town, Steve Yegge][ref_yegge_gastown]]. An agent mail system gives agents persistent identities, git-backed threaded messages, and glob-scoped advisory file reservations with expiry, and states that it does not centralize human arbitration [[MCP Agent Mail][ref_mcp_agent_mail]]. A practitioner argument for decision records adapted to agents holds that git records what changed and never why, and prescribes one-page immutable records superseded rather than edited [[Architecture Decision Records for AI Coding Agents, BrainGrid][ref_braingrid_adr]], the same form the decision-record community maintains [[Architectural Decision Records][ref_adr]] and the United Kingdom's Government Digital Service adopted across the public sector [[The architecture decision record framework, Government Digital Service][ref_govuk_adr]]. An argument for the two-person rule for agents holds that no agent should both decide and execute an irreversible action, with policy as code standing in for the second person [[The Two-Person Rule for AI Agents, Medium][ref_two_person_agents]], which is the nuclear-era rule by analogy [[Two-person rule][ref_two_person_rule]]. An approval-queue design lists the fields a queued decision needs, the action, its arguments, a risk reason, an owner, the allowed decisions, a checkpoint, a trace link, a timeout, an escalation path, and a receipt [[Approval Queues Are the Runtime for Agentic AI Workflows, Focused Labs][ref_focused_approval_queues]], and a framework's interrupt-and-resume primitive shows how a question can surface mid-run rather than at a session boundary, provided the pre-interrupt work is idempotent [[Making it easier to build human-in-the-loop agents with interrupt, LangChain][ref_langchain_interrupt]]. Thoughtworks' radar warns of cognitive debt from generated code volume and recommends a return to fundamentals [[Technology Radar, Thoughtworks][ref_thoughtworks_radar]].

**The continuous-integration sources are tool documentation and vendor posts.** GitHub's documentation states that a required check whose workflow is skipped stays pending forever while a job skipped by a condition reports success [[Troubleshooting required status checks, GitHub Docs][ref_github_required_checks]] [[Skipping workflow runs, GitHub Docs][ref_github_skipping]], that a job without a timeout runs to a six-hour default [[Workflow syntax, timeout-minutes, GitHub Docs][ref_github_timeout]], and a maintained action filters at the job level so the workflow still completes [[Paths Changes Filter, dorny][ref_paths_filter]]. The nextest documentation describes slice-based and hash-based sharding of a test binary across runners [[Partitioning test runs in CI, cargo-nextest][ref_nextest_partitioning]]. A vendor post on compiler caching cites an independent benchmark that found no configuration in which it beat a cold baseline [[Fast Rust Builds with sccache and GitHub Actions, Depot][ref_depot_sccache]], and a merge-queue vendor claims sixty to eighty percent reductions in queue minutes [[How to Cut Your GitHub Actions CI Bill, Mergify][ref_mergify_ci_bill]]. The Rust mutation tool's documentation defines caught, missed, unviable, and timeout outcomes and surfaces only the actionable two, which is the convention that scores a mutant only after it compiled and ran [[Using the results, cargo-mutants][ref_cargo_mutants]]. Google's review guide sets a one-business-day norm for review latency on the theory that slow review pressures authors toward worse code [[Code Review Developer Guide, Google Engineering Practices][ref_google_code_review]], a norm whose survival under agent output arriving many times faster than a human author's is an open question.

### Verification Research

**The research on whether models can check their own work is consistent, and it is the basis for finding eight.** Models revising their own output without an external signal frequently degrade it, a grade A result from 2024 [[Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet][research_self_correct]]. A 2025 study of thirty seven models across nine benchmarks characterizes when a model verifying another model's output pays off, grade A in design with its figures unverified beyond the abstract [[When Does Verification Pay Off, A Closer Look at LLMs as Solution Verifiers][research_llm_verifiers]]. The cross-context review study injected 150 errors into thirty artifacts and compared four review conditions over 360 reviews, finding fully isolated review at 28.6 percent F1 against 24.6 for same-session self-review, significant at p equal to 0.008, with the gap widest on critical errors and no gain from a second same-session pass, grade A on one model with synthetic errors and no human baseline [[Cross-Context Review, Improving LLM Output Quality by Separating Production and Review Sessions][research_cross_context_review]]. A rapid review of twenty three articles on models as code reviewers, paired with an experiment against human judges, is grade B and was not fetched at the primary. Framing a pull request as a security improvement changes a reviewer's detection rate, and metadata redaction partly mitigates it, grade A [[Measuring and Exploiting Contextual Bias in LLM-Assisted Security Code Review][research_security_review_bias]]. A study of GitHub's reviewer on security flaws finds detection dropping sharply for privilege escalation, architectural, and business-logic issues, grade A [[Evaluating GitHub Copilot Code Review on Security Flaws][research_copilot_security]]. A benchmark converting SWE-bench issue and pull request pairs into review tasks finds a substantial detection gap even for strong models, grade A with per-tool figures unverified [[CR-Bench, Evaluating the Real-World Utility of AI Code Review Agents][research_cr_bench]]. A three-role protocol of author, reviewer, and critic of the reviewer beat a single agent on SWE-bench Verified and underperformed a simpler baseline on a harder benchmark until disagreement was required to carry explicit evidence, at about four and a half times the tokens, with false consensus as its named failure mode, grade A [[Adversarial Review, Structured Disagreement for Grounded Agentic Code Review][research_adversarial_review]]. The largest evaluation of models as judges, twenty one models over about 541,000 judgments, finds raw agreement overstating discriminative ability by thirty four to forty one points after chance correction and a consistency-bias paradox in which reproducibility above 0.95 coexists with severe positional bias, grade A [[Reliability without Validity, A Systematic, Large-Scale Evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge Models][research_judge_reliability]].

**On tests, the research bounds what a model-written check can be trusted to mean.** Model-generated mutants score above 0.7 against rule-based tools' roughly 0.5, grade A [[A Comprehensive Study on Large Language Models for Mutation Testing][research_llm_mutation]], but for suites written by models, size correlates only weakly with mutation score and mutation score is a weaker proxy for effectiveness than for human-written suites, grade A [[Do Coverage and Mutation Scores of LLM-Generated Test Suites Correlate with Their Effectiveness? A Replicability Study][research_mutation_replicability]]. Agent-written property-based tests catch thirty to forty percent of seeded bugs against a human baseline of seventy to eighty, failing by over-constraining or under-constraining, grade A [[PBT-Bench, Benchmarking AI Agents on Property-Based Testing][research_pbt_bench]]. Agents synthesizing input generators and grammars for coverage-guided fuzzers have found bugs in compilers and libraries that traditional fuzzers missed, grade A per study [[Fuzzing with Agents, Generators Are All You Need][research_fuzzing_agents]], and the differential-testing literature, including an agentic triage system for divergences, is explicit that the oracle exposes a divergence only on inputs the corpus or generator produces, grade B to C [[SmartOracle, Agentic Triage for Differential Testing][research_smartoracle]]. Metamorphic testing was searched for and no controlled study of it on generated code was found.

### Context and Memory Research

**Two controlled results establish why a large resume surface hurts, and one case study establishes a remedy.** Retrieval across long inputs follows a U shape, highest at the start and end and degrading in the middle even for models built for long contexts, grade A [[Lost in the Middle, How Language Models Use Long Contexts][research_lost_in_the_middle]]. Across eighteen frontier models, accuracy degrades non-uniformly and often well before the stated limit, sometimes by thirty to fifty percent, with lower similarity between question and answer accelerating the decline, grade A with a public replication toolkit [[Context Rot, Chroma Research][ref_chroma_context_rot]]. A case study across 391 consecutive sessions of a model-managed documentation set names index sickness, in which structure, identifiers, and rules added to the set lead the model to reason inside the symbolic layer rather than about the system, and reports that separating a compact baseline document from an append-only log cut instruction volume by about three quarters with no recurrence over the next 150 sessions, grade B as a single self-reported case [[Written by AI, Managed by AI, Semantic Space Control and Index Sickness Elimination Across 391 Consecutive Sessions][research_index_sickness]].

### Process and Measurement Literature

**The measurement literature predates the tools and still governs what a progress figure means.** The four keys, deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, and time to restore, validated over a decade and more than 39,000 professionals, established that elite performers achieve speed and stability together, grade B [[DORA metrics history][ref_dora_four_keys]]. The 2021 paper on the SPACE of developer productivity, whose name is an acronym for satisfaction, performance, activity, communication, and efficiency, established that no single metric captures productivity and that counts of output are activity measures, grade A [[The SPACE of Developer Productivity][research_space]]. The DX Core 4 separates speed from effectiveness, quality, and impact, with a reported deployment across 3,500 engineers, grade B [[Introducing the DX Core 4, Abi Noda][ref_dx_core4]]. Pre-registration, declaring the analysis before running it, has a methodological literature of its own [[Pre-registration for Predictive Modeling][research_preregistration_modeling]] [[Preregistration][ref_preregistration]], and it is the basis for the prediction-before-measurement practice the V0.3.X line already keeps. Exit criteria and definitions of done are decades-old consensus, grade D [[Exit criteria][ref_exit_criteria]]. A pattern catalogue for approval processes names about fourteen patterns including delegated and default decisions, grade C, and was not readable at the primary [[Design Patterns for Approval Processes][research_approval_patterns]]. The definitions the equations use are the harmonic mean of precision and recall [[F-score][ref_f_score]], Cohen's chance-corrected agreement [[Cohen's kappa][ref_cohens_kappa]] [[A Coefficient of Agreement for Nominal Scales][research_cohen_1960]], the serial-fraction bound on speedup [[Amdahl's law][ref_amdahl]] [[Validity of the single processor approach to achieving large scale computing capabilities][research_amdahl_1967]], and a tokenizer's rule of thumb [[What are tokens and how to count them, OpenAI][ref_openai_tokens]].

Anthropic's June 2026 analysis of four hundred thousand sessions confirms the 70 percent planning and 20 percent execution split for users [[Agentic coding and persistent returns to expertise, Anthropic][ref_anthropic_expertise]]. And the Sakana workshop paper was withdrawn by a protocol agreed in advance with the conference, not for misconduct, so it is evidence that an autonomous pipeline can pass a review gate and not evidence that it failed one [[The AI Scientist Generates its First Peer-Reviewed Scientific Publication, Sakana AI][ref_sakana_first_publication]] [[The AI Scientist-v2, Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search][research_ai_scientist_v2]].

**Four things the field has not established bear directly on this project.** No study evaluates the combination this project uses, several concurrent lines with worktree isolation, markdown mailboxes read through version control, a release-branch gate, and fresh-context adversarial audit, as a system. No source gives a portable defect catch rate for fresh-context review, only the one controlled comparison above. No source reports rework or revert rates for autonomous branches merged after an automated gate, which is the measure this project would need in order to know rather than assume that its gate is sufficient.

**The literature's most relevant warning is the perception gap.** The METR result is that developers misjudged their own speed by about forty percentage points in real time [[Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity, METR][ref_metr_study]]. The project's record contains the same shape in miniature, a residency figure believed refuted by a factor of forty that was in fact correct, a sweep believed to be measuring coverage that was measuring exit codes, a handoff believed current that was three days stale. The remedy the literature suggests and the project's own best increments already practice is to state the prediction before the measurement and record both.

## The State of the Art, by Finding

**The second research pass looked for techniques rather than for evidence about outcomes, and graded each source on the same scale.** The sources are in four evidence files beside this draft. What follows is the mapping from each finding to the best-supported techniques the pass found, with the grade and the main cost or failure mode, and with a note where the project already has the technique in a weaker form.

**For the resume surface, finding one, the strongest evidence is about why large files hurt and the best-matched practice is about how to bound them.** Two controlled studies, the 2024 lost-in-the-middle result and Chroma's 2025 context-rot benchmark over eighteen models, establish that retrieval degrades in the middle of long inputs and that accuracy falls non-uniformly well before a model's stated limit, sometimes by a third to a half [[Lost in the Middle, How Language Models Use Long Contexts][research_lost_in_the_middle]] [[Context Rot, Chroma Research][ref_chroma_context_rot]]. Both are grade A. They say why a session that reads a megabyte at startup is less reliable, not how large a document should be. The practice evidence is grade B and C. A 2026 case study over 391 consecutive sessions of a model-managed documentation set names the failure the audit found, structure and rules and identifiers added until the model reasons inside the symbolic layer rather than about the system, and reports that physically separating a compact baseline document from an append-only log cut instruction volume by three quarters and prevented recurrence over the next 150 sessions [[Written by AI, Managed by AI, Semantic Space Control and Index Sickness Elimination Across 391 Consecutive Sessions][research_index_sickness]]. Two shipping products enforce a bound. Claude Code loads only the first two hundred lines or twenty five kilobytes of its own memory index and warns and then errors when a write nears the cap, so the index gets rewritten rather than grown, and Cursor recommends five hundred lines per rule file and splitting above it [[How Claude remembers your project, Claude Code documentation][ref_claude_code_memory]] [[Rules, Cursor documentation][ref_cursor_rules]]. Anthropic's guidance for long-running agents prescribes a small progress file plus git history as the durable state, and Manus documents recitation, a small state file rewritten in place and re-read each turn [[Effective harnesses for long-running agents, Anthropic][ref_anthropic_harnesses]] [[Context Engineering for AI Agents, Manus][ref_manus_context]] [[Ralph Wiggum as a software engineer, Geoffrey Huntley][ref_ralph]] . The project has the append-only log already. It lacks the compact baseline and the enforced cap, and the one cap it did impose, on the reverse prompt in July, worked.

**For stale figures, finding two, the pass found no technique beyond the one the project already has, and one caution.** The derive-not-copy remedy is the practice everywhere it was looked for. The caution is from the 391-session study, that a document set which grows by adding identifiers and cross-references to itself invites exactly the self-referential drift the audit measured, so the fix is not more pins in more prose but fewer figures in prose at all.

**For the instruments, finding three, the pass found that the standard tooling already guards against the exact defect the mutation harness had, and two controlled results that bound how much any agent-written check can be trusted.** The Rust mutation tool cargo-mutants treats a mutant's outcome as meaningful only after confirming the mutant compiled and ran, which is precisely the check a harness that scored any non-zero exit as detected was skipping, and this is documentation, grade C [[Using the results, cargo-mutants][ref_cargo_mutants]]. Two 2026 studies are grade A. One finds that for suites written by language models, mutation score correlates only weakly with real bug-catching effectiveness, so even a correct scorer is a soft signal here [[Do Coverage and Mutation Scores of LLM-Generated Test Suites Correlate with Their Effectiveness? A Replicability Study][research_mutation_replicability]]. The other benchmarks agent-written property-based tests against human-written ones and finds the agent properties catch thirty to forty percent of seeded bugs against seventy to eighty for humans, failing by being over-constrained or under-constrained, which is the property-testing form of a check that cannot fail [[PBT-Bench, Benchmarking AI Agents on Property-Based Testing][research_pbt_bench]]. A large 2026 evaluation of models used as judges, over half a million judgments, finds a consistency-bias paradox, judges with run-to-run reproducibility above 0.95 alongside severe positional bias, and raw agreement overstating discriminative ability by thirty to forty points [[Reliability without Validity, A Systematic, Large-Scale Evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge Models][research_judge_reliability]]. An instrument that always gives the same answer is not thereby giving the right one. Meta's production practice, grade B, keeps a human as the verifier of the verifier, accepting or rejecting each generated test, and Cursor's review tool measures its own resolution rate rather than only its flag count [[LLMs Are the Key to Mutation Testing and Better Compliance, Meta Engineering][ref_meta_mutation]] [[Mutation-Guided LLM-Based Test Generation at Meta][research_meta_ach]] [[Building a Better Bugbot, Cursor][ref_cursor_bugbot]]. The negative control the audit proposes is not named as a practice anywhere the pass looked, but the cargo-mutants convention functions as one.

**For lessons, finding four, the pass found the baseline-and-log separation again, and the architecture decision record as the nearest named container.** Decision records are one-page, versioned, immutable once accepted, and superseded rather than edited, and the United Kingdom's Government Digital Service adopted them across the public sector in December 2025, so the form is mainstream [[Architectural Decision Records][ref_adr]] [[The architecture decision record framework, Government Digital Service][ref_govuk_adr]]. Nothing in the literature uses a cause, check, and habit ledger by that or any similar name. The blog repository's verification-traps document appears to be original, which is worth knowing before proposing it.

**For rulings, finding five, the pass found one shipping mechanism that addresses the relay class directly, one controlled result on coordination architecture, and a survey that reframes the operator's role.** Claude Code's agent teams pass messages through a per-agent mailbox and tell the receiving agent that a message came from another session and not from the human, so a relayed approval is untrusted input by construction [[Orchestrate teams of Claude Code sessions, Claude Code documentation][ref_claude_code_agent_teams]]. That prevents a fabricated approval and does not create the durable artifact of the human's actual answer, which a project would still need. A Carnegie Mellon study of asynchronous engineering agents, grade A, finds that a central manager building a dependency graph and delegating to agents in isolated git worktrees with test-gated merges beats both single agents and looser multi-agent arrangements by fifteen to twenty six points on two benchmarks, and that manager is a de facto single place where a ruling would be written once [[Effective Strategies for Asynchronous Software Engineering Agents][research_caid]]. A 2026 study of 1,902 multi-agent runs, grade B, finds that designating a single coordinator produced no reliable improvement, that direct messaging grows near-quadratically with team size, and that shared files cut output tokens by two fifths at eight agents [[When Agents Coordinate, Measuring Coordination in Multi-Agent AI Coding][research_when_agents_coordinate]]. A 2026 mining study of a git-native coordination log, grade B with an open question about whether its agents were real sessions, finds that adding shared task state drove a duplicate-work rate from 78 percent to zero [[Before the Pull Request, Mining Multi-Agent Coordination][research_before_the_pr]]. And a December 2025 survey of 86 practitioners running production agents finds that 68 percent of them take ten or fewer steps before human intervention and that static workflows are preferred to open-ended autonomy for reliability [[Measuring Agents in Production][research_agents_in_production]]. The operator being the long pole is the industry norm, not a defect of this project. The approval-queue literature supplies the fields a medium decision needs, a timeout, an escalation path, and an approval receipt, and the decision-record literature supplies the packet form, options with a recommended default [[Approval Queues Are the Runtime for Agentic AI Workflows, Focused Labs][ref_focused_approval_queues]] [[Architectural Decision Records][ref_adr]] [[Design Patterns for Approval Processes][research_approval_patterns]]. The near-quadratic growth of direct messaging follows from the number of pairs among the lines, and the coordination study's goodput figures give the size of the effect of shared task state.

$$
N_{\text{pairs}} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} \qquad n = 3 \Rightarrow 3 \qquad n = 4 \Rightarrow 6 \qquad n = 8 \Rightarrow 28 \qquad \frac{G_{\text{shared}}}{G_{\text{none}}} = \frac{8.00}{2.33} \approx 3.4
$$

**With three lines the project has three channels to keep consistent, and with the audit line four it has six, which is the number of places a ruling can now be recorded differently.** The coordination study's threefold goodput gain came from shared task state, not from messaging.

**For calendar time, finding six, the pass found the mechanisms and one platform hazard that changes proposal five.** GitHub's own documentation states that a required status check whose workflow is skipped by a path filter stays pending forever, while a job skipped by a condition reports success and satisfies the requirement, so the filter must be applied at the job level, for which a maintained action exists, and not at the workflow level [[Troubleshooting required status checks, GitHub Docs][ref_github_required_checks]] [[Skipping workflow runs, GitHub Docs][ref_github_skipping]] [[Paths Changes Filter, dorny][ref_paths_filter]]. The nextest partitioning documentation gives the drop-in mechanism for sharding one test binary across runners [[Partitioning test runs in CI, cargo-nextest][ref_nextest_partitioning]]. A vendor post on sccache cites an independent benchmark finding no configuration in which it beat a cold baseline, so caching should be measured on this project before being trusted [[Fast Rust Builds with sccache and GitHub Actions, Depot][ref_depot_sccache]]. The platform's default job timeout is six hours and the fix is one line [[Workflow syntax, timeout-minutes, GitHub Docs][ref_github_timeout]]. Merge-queue vendors claim 60 to 80 percent reductions in queue minutes, grade C and an upper bound [[How to Cut Your GitHub Actions CI Bill, Mergify][ref_mergify_ci_bill]]. The DORA 2025 capabilities model names small batch size as a predictor of whether adoption of these tools helps, and an unverified industry study finds that once pull request size is controlled for, authorship by a model has negligible independent effect on defect rate, which bears on the project's peers with pull requests 154 percent larger more than on the project itself, whose median pull request is 265 lines [[AI Capabilities Model, DORA][ref_dora_capabilities]].

**For progress measurement, finding seven, the pass found the frameworks and one result that validates a practice the project already has.** The 2021 paper on the SPACE of developer productivity, a five-dimension framework whose name is an acronym for satisfaction, performance, activity, communication, and efficiency, grade A, establishes that a single metric cannot capture productivity and that merge and test counts are activity metrics in its terms [[The SPACE of Developer Productivity][research_space]] . DX Core 4 separates speed from effectiveness, quality, and impact and has a reported deployment at Booking.com over 3,500 engineers, grade B [[Introducing the DX Core 4, Abi Noda][ref_dx_core4]]. The pre-registration literature, grade A, is the methodological basis for the V0.3.X line's practice of stating a predicted test-count movement before each absorption and recording the actual afterward, and it suggests extending the prediction from a number to a process [[Pre-registration for Predictive Modeling][research_preregistration_modeling]] [[Preregistration][ref_preregistration]]. Exit criteria and definitions of done are decades-old consensus, grade D, and the absence of a written lifting criterion for the release is a finding-five problem in finding-seven clothing, since someone has to commit to a threshold in writing [[Exit criteria][ref_exit_criteria]].

**For verification, finding eight, the pass found the single most useful result in the whole research effort, and a product that already implements the proposal.** A 2026 controlled study of cross-context review, grade A, injected 150 errors into thirty artifacts and compared same-session self-review, repeated same-session review, subagent review that could see the generation prompt, and fully isolated review with no access to the production conversation, three trials each. Isolated review reached an F1 of 28.6 percent against 24.6 for self-review, significant at p equal to 0.008, with the gap widest on critical errors at forty percent caught against twenty nine. A second same-session pass gave no improvement, which rules out the explanation that reviewing twice helps [[Cross-Context Review, Improving LLM Output Quality by Separating Production and Review Sessions][research_cross_context_review]]. The study is one model, synthetic errors, and no human baseline, so it is evidence that context separation is the active ingredient and not a portable catch rate. It agrees with the 2024 result, grade A, that models cannot self-correct reasoning without an external signal [[Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet][research_self_correct]]. On tools, a vendor benchmark of five reviewers against fifty real bugs reports catch rates from 44 to 82 percent, a third-party benchmark over three hundred thousand real pull requests reports the best tool at 51 percent F1 with precision near fifty, so half of what a reviewer flags is not acted on, and a security study finds automated review reliably catches shallow bugs and reliably misses cross-file and architectural ones [[AI Code Review Benchmarks, Greptile][ref_greptile_benchmarks]] [[CodeRabbit tops independent AI code review benchmark, CodeRabbit][ref_coderabbit_martian]] [[Evaluating GitHub Copilot Code Review on Security Flaws][research_copilot_security]] [[Measuring and Exploiting Contextual Bias in LLM-Assisted Security Code Review][research_security_review_bias]]. A 2026 adversarial protocol with an author, a reviewer, and a critic of the reviewer, grade A, beat a single agent on SWE-bench Verified but underperformed a simpler baseline on a harder benchmark until disagreement was forced to carry explicit evidence, and its named failure mode is false consensus, the critic yielding to a confident reviewer [[Adversarial Review, Structured Disagreement for Grounded Agentic Code Review][research_adversarial_review]]. Anthropic ships a multi-agent code review for Claude Code in research preview whose design checks each candidate finding against actual code behavior before posting it, and reports internally that under one percent of findings are marked incorrect [[Code Review for Claude Code, Anthropic][ref_anthropic_code_review]]. The proposal to trial fresh-context review at merge therefore has grade A support for its mechanism, a shipping implementation to try before building one, and a known failure mode to measure for. Two definitions make the review figures comparable. The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, and the chance-corrected agreement the judge study uses is Cohen's kappa [[F-score][ref_f_score]] [[Cohen's kappa][ref_cohens_kappa]] [[A Coefficient of Agreement for Nominal Scales][research_cohen_1960]].

$$
F_{1} = \frac{2PR}{P + R} \qquad \kappa = \frac{p_{o} - p_{e}}{1 - p_{e}}
$$

**The cross-context result is a four point rise in F1, from 24.6 to 28.6, which is a relative gain of sixteen percent from context separation alone, and the judge study's finding is that raw agreement overstates kappa by thirty four to forty one points.** A reviewer at 49 percent precision produces one unactionable flag for every actionable one, and an adversarial protocol at four and a half times the tokens of a single pass must catch enough to pay for itself.

## The Shape of the Trade-off

**The operator's framing was that the four objectives pull in different directions and that the shape might be a triangle or something else.** On the evidence, the shape has two regimes, and the audit states this as an inference rather than a measurement.

**In the first regime the objectives do not trade off against one another.** Every mechanizable weakness in the record, a stale figure, a fabricating instrument, a wait loop that cannot terminate, a documentation-only run through a twenty two job matrix, a ruling that reaches a line by relay, costs all four objectives at once. It lowers quality, it makes the progress record dishonest, it burns calendar time on rework, and it consumes operator attention on the escalation that follows. Repairing it structurally improves all four, though each repair costs work to build and a ruling to adopt. This matches the DORA finding that speed and stability are achieved together by teams that invest in the mechanisms, with the caveat that the DORA result is a survey correlation over human teams whose applicability to one human and several agents is not established, so the alignment claim rests first on the project's own record [[DORA metrics history][ref_dora_four_keys]]. There is no triangle here. There is a list of mechanisms not yet built. Writing the four objectives as quality, honesty, calendar time, and operator involvement, a mechanizable weakness moves all four the wrong way, and its repair moves all four the right way.

$$
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial w} < 0 \qquad \frac{\partial H}{\partial w} < 0 \qquad \frac{\partial T}{\partial w} > 0 \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial w} > 0
$$

**There is no direction in which reducing such a weakness costs any objective, which is what it means to say the four are aligned in this regime.**

**In the second regime there is a genuine trade-off, and it has one edge.** The decisions that cannot be mechanized are the ones the process reserves for the operator, semantics, irreversible actions, breaking interfaces, publication. For those, the only ways to reduce operator involvement are to give each decision a default that a session may act on after a stated interval, or to accept that the work behind the decision waits. A default moves risk onto the sessions in exactly the cases where the record shows their judgement is least reliable, and the record's worst incident, a publish on a misread authorization, is in this category [[RELEASE_PROCESS.md at 802e72d3][primary_release_process]] [[Two-person rule][ref_two_person_rule]]. The proofs appendix incident shows the two regimes touching. The relay itself is mechanizable by a ledger, but what it relayed was a topology decision only the operator could make, and the operator made it three ways in three sessions. The trade-off is therefore between operator involvement and calendar time on the largest items, mediated by the quality of the decision packet the sessions prepare and by whether the operator's answer is written once where every line can read it. A well-prepared packet, options, recommendation, default, and the tree read first, costs the operator minutes. A single question at a session boundary costs a day. A default with an interval is admissible when the expected cost of the default being wrong is below the cost of waiting, and the expected cost is the probability the default is wrong times the cost of reversing it.

$$
\mathbb{E}[C_{\text{default}}] = p_{\text{wrong}} \, C_{\text{revert}} \qquad C_{\text{wait}} = \lambda \, L \qquad \text{admissible when } p_{\text{wrong}} \, C_{\text{revert}} < \lambda \, L
$$

**For an irreversible action the reversal cost is unbounded and no default is admissible at any latency, which is the process's existing rule.** For a medium decision the inequality is decidable, and the record supplies the terms, a twenty three day wait on one side and a revert that needed a third commit and an absorption on the other.

**The shape, then, is not a triangle.** It is a large region in which the four objectives are aligned and improvable by mechanism, bounded by one edge along which the operator's attention is exchanged for the latency of irreversible decisions. The proposals below are sorted accordingly. The first group needs a ruling to adopt and trades no objective against another. The second group is the edge, and each proposal on it states what it moves and to whom.

## Proposals

**Each proposal names its mechanism, the evidence it rests on, its expected effect on the four objectives, and the ruling it requires.** None is adopted by this document. Effects are the auditor's expectation and are marked as such.

### Group One, Mechanisms That Trade No Objective Against Another

**Proposal one, bound the resume surface.** Each line carries one current-state document of at most two hundred lines, containing only what a resuming session must know, with every historical section moved to an archive that the startup protocol does not read. The task log and the handoff are split the way the reverse prompt was split from the journal in July, and a test fails when the current-state document exceeds its bound. The design journal is rotated monthly. The archive keeps the record this audit mined, and nothing is deleted. Evidence is finding one, the project's own July precedent, the two grade A context-degradation results, the 391-session baseline-and-log case study, and the enforced cap Claude Code applies to its own memory index [[How Claude remembers your project, Claude Code documentation][ref_claude_code_memory]]. Expected effect is a shorter and more reliable resume on every session, which is calendar time and honesty, and no cost to quality or operator involvement. Ruling required is on the bound and on the archive location.

**Proposal two, derive every figure and never write one.** A script emits the figures that the root instruction file, the handoffs, and the mailboxes currently carry in prose, and the existing claimed-counts test is generalized so that any figure in a governed document that disagrees with its derivation fails the suite. Documents cite the derivation by name rather than restating its output. Evidence is finding two, nineteen incidents, and the two existing tests that already do this for two figures. Expected effect is the removal of the largest failure class, which is quality and honesty, and less operator time spent on corrections. Ruling required is the list of governed documents.

**Proposal three, a lesson ships with a mechanism or is labelled a habit.** A single lessons ledger, modelled on the blog's verification-traps document, holds one entry per recorded incident with its cause, the check that would have caught it, and the mechanism that now does, or the explicit label that no mechanism exists and the entry is a habit. A test pins that every entry has one or the other. The thirteen reactive rules from finding four seed it, and entries take the decision-record form, one page, versioned, superseded rather than edited. Evidence is finding four, the frozen-measurement history, and the decision-record practice, with the caveat that the cause, check, and habit form is not documented anywhere outside the blog repository. Expected effect is that recurrence becomes visible as a ledger entry with the habit label, which is honesty, and that the seven sentence-form rules acquire mechanisms over time, which is quality. Ruling required is adoption.

**Proposal four, a negative control for every checker.** Any new test, script, or probe that asserts the absence of something ships with a control that must fire, and the pull request template asks for it by name. The project already does this in its best increments, mutation-tested three ways, a must-fire control, proved in both directions, and the standard mutation tool's convention of scoring only mutants that compiled and ran is the same discipline. Evidence is finding three, nine tabulated incidents, most silent, and the two grade A results that agent-written checks catch about half of what human-written ones do and that a judge's consistency is not its validity [[PBT-Bench, Benchmarking AI Agents on Property-Based Testing][research_pbt_bench]] [[Reliability without Validity, A Systematic, Large-Scale Evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge Models][research_judge_reliability]]. Expected effect is quality and honesty, at a small cost in calendar time per increment. Ruling required is adoption.

**Proposal five, a documentation path filter that keeps the document-reading tests, and a sharded test job.** The workflow skips most of the test matrix for pull requests that touch only documentation, keeping the link check, the documentation build, and the test binaries that read documents, the claimed-counts test, the documentation-links test, and the handoff boundary test, since a filter that skipped those would reproduce the 2026-09-03 blind spot at the workflow level. The filter is applied per job with a maintained path-filter action and not per workflow, because a required check whose workflow is skipped stays pending forever under the platform's documented semantics. The default-features test job is partitioned across runners so its wall clock stops tracking suite growth. A job timeout is set so a stuck run fails in an hour rather than six. The test job's duration is recorded per run as a canary. Evidence is finding six and finding two. Expected effect is calendar time, most of a run per documentation pull request and a bounded merge latency for code, with no cost to the other three. Ruling required is on the filter's definition of documentation-only and on which test binaries count as document-reading, since the project's own record shows a documentation commit that swept in a test file.

**Proposal six, a rulings ledger on the trunk, with the packet as the unit of asking.** The V0.3.X line's decisions directory already holds the right artifact, a rulings file with verbatim quotes, dates, and the receiving line's reading marked as its own, and an open-decisions page with option tables. It is moved to the trunk, every line reads it and no line acts on a ruling that is not in it, and questions to the operator are batched into packets at session boundaries, each with options, a recommendation, and the tree read first. Evidence is finding five, ten relayed-ruling incidents, the packet that delivered seven rulings and one relay error, the shipping agent-teams design that treats a peer's message as untrusted, the controlled result favoring a single place where work and decisions are recorded, and the approval-queue and decision-record forms [[Orchestrate teams of Claude Code sessions, Claude Code documentation][ref_claude_code_agent_teams]]. Expected effect is operator involvement, fewer and better-prepared decisions, and honesty, since the relay class disappears. Ruling required is the ledger's location and the rule that the ledger is authoritative.

**Proposal seven, a roadmap ledger with checkable criteria.** The five success criteria for the release line, and the equivalent for the backend line, are written as items whose status a script or test reports, and the release hold is given a written lifting criterion, even if that criterion is a date or a decision rather than a test. Evidence is finding seven, the result that activity counts alone are an incomplete signal, and the pre-registration literature behind the prediction practice the project already has. Expected effect is honesty, and calendar time, since the sessions can work toward a stated criterion. Ruling required is the criteria themselves, which only the operator can state.

### Group Two, the Edge

**Proposal eight, defaults with intervals for bounded decisions.** For each open decision in the ledger the operator may attach a default and an interval, after which a session proceeds on the default and records that it did. The audit recommends this only for decisions that are reversible by a single revert, and explicitly not for publication, format version bumps, opcode additions, or breaking public interfaces, which stay at an explicit word. Evidence is finding five and the two worst incidents in the record. Expected effect is calendar time on medium items, at the cost of operator control over them, and the risk that a default is wrong is bounded by reversibility. Reversibility must be judged after absorption, since a revert on a shared trunk has needed a third commit rather than an amend, and must then propagate through the numbered absorptions into the other trunk. Ruling required is per decision, which is the point.

**Proposal nine, fresh-context review of code at merge, measured before it binds.** A reviewer session with no shared context reads each pull request and files findings that the author dispositions before merge, in parallel with the continuous-integration run so it costs no calendar time. For thirty days the review is advisory and its findings are recorded with their disposition, so that the project has a catch rate before deciding whether to make it binding. The trial should try the vendor's shipping multi-agent review before building a bespoke one, should require that any disagreement between reviewer and author carry explicit evidence, and should watch for false consensus. Evidence is finding eight, the proof audit's results, the grade A cross-context review result that isolation itself raises detection, and the grade A adversarial-review result that naive disagreement can underperform [[Cross-Context Review, Improving LLM Output Quality by Separating Production and Review Sessions][research_cross_context_review]] [[Adversarial Review, Structured Disagreement for Grounded Agentic Code Review][research_adversarial_review]] [[Code Review for Claude Code, Anthropic][ref_anthropic_code_review]]. Expected effect is quality, at a token cost and a small operator cost to read the thirty-day summary. Ruling required is whether to run the trial.

**Proposal ten, fewer absorptions or a single trunk.** The V0.3.X line has absorbed the V0.2.X line fifty four times, each a merge with a predicted and measured test-count movement. The discipline is excellent and the count is a cost. Whether the two trunks should remain two, absorb on a schedule rather than continuously, or collapse into one with the backend behind a feature flag is a question the audit raises rather than answers, and the coordination literature offers one relevant number, a 27.67 percent merge-conflict rate over 107,000 replayed agent-authored pull requests, and one relevant null result, that a designated coordinator did not reliably help in a study of 1,902 runs, because it depends on the release plan for the two versions, which the parallel-development document explicitly defers to the operator [[When Agents Coordinate, Measuring Coordination in Multi-Agent AI Coding][research_when_agents_coordinate]] [[AgenticFlict, A Large-Scale Dataset of Merge Conflicts in Agentic Pull Requests][research_agenticflict]]. Expected effect of any change is calendar time and coordination, at a risk to the isolation the two trunks provide. Ruling required is the release question itself. The absorption cadence is the count over the life of the second trunk.

$$
r_{\text{absorb}} = \frac{54}{30\,\text{days}} = 1.8\,\text{per day}
$$

**The second trunk has absorbed the first almost twice a day since it was cut on 2026-08-08, each absorption a merge with a predicted and a measured test-count movement.** The discipline is excellent and the count is a cost, and the coordination literature's null result on coordinators and its 27.67 percent conflict rate over agent-authored pull requests are the two figures a ruling would weigh.

### Observations Without a Proposal

**Three things were measured and are recorded without a recommendation.** The main branch has ninety failed workflow runs against ninety five successful ones, all failures between 2026-05-08 and 2026-07-11 under the earlier trunk-based model, with no run on main since 2026-07-24. That is a historical record of a period in which pushes to the release branch failed continuous integration about half the time, and it is closed by the release-branch model, but the contributor guide still describes the old model. Six feature branches sit unmerged into both trunks with last commits between 2026-08-09 and 2026-08-14, all native-backend spikes, and whether they are abandoned or queued is not determinable from the branch list. And the audit's own first attempt was terminated by a session limit the process documents do not mention, which is a constraint on calendar time that a line planning heavy parallel work should budget for by running mining and audit passes on smaller models and long runs in the background with their exit status captured.

## The Source Base

**The engineering evidence was read from three branches through version control, never from a working directory another session was using.** The V0.2.X line's documents were read at commit `802e72d3`, the cut point of the audit branch. The V0.3.X line's were read at `b8d78c3f` and its mailbox was re-read live because a scratch export proved one hundred four lines stale against the tip during the pass, which is itself a small instance of finding two. The proofs line's were read at `6537a36f`. The git and workflow record was measured by commands recorded in the fourth evidence file, with raw exports beside it. The blog repository was read on its master branch at `68182f3`, plus one untracked draft in its drafts directory [[_verify.py, sgeos.github.io at 68182f3][primary_blog_verify]].

**The miners were instructed to label every claim executed or read, and their reports do so.** The auditor checked the miners' figures for the document sizes, the main-branch failure window, the missing job timeout, the pull request and workflow totals, and the net line counts by bucket, recounted the incident classes over the tabulated rows, and did not re-run the keyword sweeps or re-derive the classification of any row, which remains the miners' work. The external surveys' sources were fetched by the survey agents and graded by them. The auditor did not re-fetch them. Five primary sources the first survey marked unverified were fetched in the second pass, and their figures are reported above with the residual caveat that the Cui field experiment was reached only through summaries of its abstract.

## Epistemic State

**What is measured.** The document sizes, the commit and pull request and workflow counts, the cycle times, the job durations on a five-run sample, the prefix classification of titles and subjects, the branch counts, and the main-branch failure window are executed measurements from commands whose text and output are preserved.

**What is read.** Every incident in findings two through five is the project's own record of itself, reproduced. The audit did not reproduce any incident. The classification into root-cause classes is the miners' judgement over the project's prose and is a lower bound.

**What is inferred.** The two-regime shape of the trade-off, the expected effect of each proposal, the claim that no human review sits between a green run and a merge, and the claim that the difference between sentence-form and mechanism-form rules lies in how their recurrence is detected are inferences from the record. The last rests on thirteen rules and a handful of recurrences and is suggestive rather than established.

**What is uncertain.** The seventy three untitled pull requests were not coded, so the documentation share is a floor, and no pull request was checked by file list. The pull request figures cover twenty eight days. The job-duration growth rests on five samples. The incident tables are a sample of a larger keyword sweep and their per-row classes are the miners' judgement.

**What could not be verified.** The external surveys' sources were not re-fetched by the auditor. The Cui field experiment's text was reached only through summaries of its abstract. Several technique sources are 2026 preprints whose peer-review status is unconfirmed, and the study reporting a 78 percent duplicate-work rate does not say whether its agents were real sessions. The Faros, Carnegie Mellon, Anthropic usage, and Sakana sources were fetched in the second pass and are first hand.

**What the audit excludes.** It did not read source code, did not run any build or test beyond two documentation tests on its own branch, did not evaluate the correctness of any engineering decision, and did not assess the proof.

## Out of Scope

**Whether the project should exist in its current shape, whether the self-hosting goal is worth its cost, and whether two trunks should be one are release and roadmap questions that the operator owns.** The audit measured their process cost and stopped there. The blog repository's own process was read only as a comparison point and a format reference, and no finding about it is proposed for action.

## Conclusion

**The project has built, by hand and under pressure, most of the discipline that the literature recommends.** Prediction before measurement. Fresh-context adversarial audit. A byte-identical differential oracle. Provenance labels on every claim. A refusal to record a relayed ruling as settled. What it has not done is turn its own lessons into mechanisms at the rate it learns them, and the cost of that gap is visible in the same record that shows the learning. The largest failure class is a figure written into prose. The most expensive incidents are instruments that reported what they did not measure. The longest waits are single questions asked one at a time. Each of those has a mechanism, and none of the mechanisms trades quality, honesty, calendar time, or operator attention against one another. The one real trade-off, operator attention against latency on irreversible decisions, is narrow, and its lever is the quality of the packet the sessions prepare before they ask.

**The audit's recommendation is to adopt the first group as a unit and to rule on the second group item by item.** The first group is bounded work for a single line, its duration is not estimated here, and it would be measured by its own ledgers thereafter. The second group is the operator's, and the audit has tried to make each item on it cheap to decide.
