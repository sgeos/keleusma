COMPLETION CONDITION — Order-1 gate vector strength

Ordering is explicitly NOT a completion criterion. Any order satisfies this.

All of the following must hold as observable properties of the tree.

1. The Order-1 gate report states how many argument vectors its
   agreeing stages were driven at, derived from the runs actually
   performed rather than from a constant or an assumption.

2. If those stages received only one vector each, the tree says so
   plainly. A weak result is reported as weak and is not softened,
   omitted, or described in terms implying many vectors.

3. No figure in that report can be read as qualifying a population it
   did not measure. Every count names the set it was taken over, so a
   reader does not depend on proximity or on a relative word to tell
   which report a number belongs to.

4. A guard fails if the stage-vector figure is derived from an empty
   stage set, or if the classification skips stages it claims to cover.
   The guard does not pin the figure to a constant, which would fail on
   ordinary corpus growth.

5. No guard asserts merely that two reported numbers differ. Distinct
   populations may legitimately share a value.

6. The resume document for this line, wherever it quotes the Order-1
   gate, is consistent with what the report measures, and states the
   gate's strength rather than only its headline count.

7. The tree does not claim the Order-1 gate is met.

8. Any claim that a figure changed is supported by a recorded
   measurement of both the before and after values, not by inference.

9. The files recorded as owned by the other line are unmodified.

10. The native code generator's suite passes with zero failures and its
    formatting and lint checks pass, with the pass/fail judgement taken
    from something other than a truncated or piped view of the output.

11. Work that is finished is committed. Pushing may be deferred while a
    pull request of this line is mid-verification, provided the
    deferral is stated and the work is not left uncommitted.
