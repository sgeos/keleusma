COMPLETION CONDITION — composite entry parameters

Ordering is explicitly NOT a completion criterion. Any order satisfies this.

All of the following must hold as observable properties of the tree.

1. Corpus modules exempt solely because their entry point takes a composite
   parameter are either driven and compared against the virtual machine, or the
   tree records why they still cannot be, with the obstacle named.

2. If they are driven, the count of executed-and-agreeing modules rises and the
   exempt count falls correspondingly. Both figures come from a run.

3. Both sides receive byte-identical composite bodies. The tree states how that
   is ensured rather than assuming it.

4. The tree states whether the supplied body VARIES across argument vectors. If
   it does not, that is reported plainly as comparison-once rather than described
   in terms that imply many vectors.

5. The tree states that these modules were already covered by another harness,
   so the gain is not presented as coverage appearing from nothing.

6. Any module newly driven must AGREE. A disagreement is reported and
   investigated, not masked or re-exempted.

7. The exemption remains in force for any composite entry shape the harness
   cannot construct. Nothing is admitted by guessing a layout.

8. The change is shown to be load-bearing by a recorded mutation: the transcript
   states what was changed, that it failed or produced a different result, and
   that the revert was verified rather than assumed.

9. Every figure stated in the resume document for this line matches a run, and
   its stated test count matches the suite.

10. The files recorded as owned by the other line are unmodified.

11. The native code generator's suite passes with zero failures and its
    formatting and lint checks pass, with the pass/fail judgement taken from
    something other than a truncated or piped view of the output.

12. Work that is finished is committed and pushed. Nothing required above is
    left only in the working tree.
