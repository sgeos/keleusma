COMPLETION CONDITION — the panic-site census

Ordering is NOT a completion criterion. Any order of the work satisfies this, and
nothing here requires a particular branch, commit shape, or process step.

All of the following hold in the tree:

1. THE SITES ARE ENUMERATED BY A MATCHER, NOT BY HAND.
   Every panic-capable construct in the emitter that encodes an assumption about
   the module is found mechanically, and the enumeration fails when the
   population changes, asking for the new site to be dispositioned rather than
   for the number to be edited.

2. EACH SITE HAS A DISPOSITION.
   For each, the tree records whether a module the public lowering entry point
   accepts can reach it, and what a caller would observe if it fired. A site
   whose reachability is unknown is recorded as unknown rather than assumed
   unreachable.

3. ANYTHING FOUND REACHABLE IS RESOLVED.
   A site a malformed module can reach either refuses instead of panicking, or is
   recorded as reachable-and-accepted with a stated reason.

4. THE SCOPE EXCLUSION IS STATED.
   The census says what it deliberately does not cover and why, rather than
   applying the exclusion silently. Its matcher is shown not to swallow the
   infallible-API calls that make up the bulk of the file's panic-capable
   constructs.

5. IT IS NON-VACUOUS.
   The enumeration is shown to find the sites already known, including the one
   converted in the previous increment.

6. NO NEW OPCODE, NO BYTECODE_VERSION CHANGE, AND NO EDIT TO src/ OR tests/ AT
   THE REPOSITORY ROOT.

7. THE BACKEND SUITE IS GREEN IN BOTH FLOAT CONFIGURATIONS, with counts
   re-derived rather than quoted.
